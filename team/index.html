Meet our team

The MCLab brings together researchers and trainees with diverse backgrounds, expertise, and perspectives.

Lab Head

Meng-Chuan Lai

Meng-Chuan Lai [賴孟泉] is a staff psychiatrist and senior scientist at the Centre for Addiction and Mental Health, Toronto, Ontario, Canada. He is an Associate Professor and Co-Chair, Advisory Council for Equity, Diversity, Inclusion, Indigeneity and Accessibility in Clinical Care at the Department of Psychiatry, and Graduate Faculty at the Institute of Medical Science and Department of Psychology, University of Toronto. He is an INSAR Fellow and an editor of the journals Autism and Molecular Autism and sits on the editorial board of the Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry. Dr. Lai received his MD from the National Taiwan University and completed psychiatry residency as well as child and adolescent psychiatry subspecialty training at the National Taiwan University Hospital. He holds a PhD in psychiatry and neuroscience from the University of Cambridge, where he also conducted post-doctoral research in autism neuroscience. Dr. Lai’s research is supported by multiple federal and international research grants alongside extensive national and international collaborations, focusing on autism and neurodivergence, sex and gender, and mental health.

Adeline Lacroix

Postdoctoral Researcher

I investigate sex-related differences in autism, with a particular focus on autistic females and the heterogeneity of autistic presentations. Her research combines neuroimaging, cognitive neuroscience, participatory approaches, and large-scale datasets to better understand neurodiversity and improve autism identification and support.

Diana Peragine

Postdoctoral Researcher

Diana examines sexual development through a biopsychosocial lens, asking how sexual inequality is written on the body. Her early PhD work used an animal model with the strictest social and reproductive hierarchy among mammals to reveal the neuroendocrine pathway by which social marginalization can block puberty. Her current PhD work examined human sexual development from a privilege perspective, acknowledging that gender inequality extends from the boardroom to the bedroom. Her other projects examine the origins of sexual and gender diversity in women and queer people who experience sex-based marginalization.

Ingrid Tien

Postdoctoral Researcher

Hello! I am a BRIDGES-funded postdoctoral research fellow at the Centre for Addiction & Mental Health, studying sex and gender-based diagnostic and service disparities in autism, the intersection of autistic and gender diverse identities, mental health trajectories of autistic youth, and neuroaffirming autism research and co-production. I recently received my PhD (2025) from UCLA under Jeffrey Wood, where I coordinated a community-based evidence-based treatment trial for autistic youth. Employing both quantitative and qualitative methodology, I have published over a dozen peer reviewed articles. Julie Lee

PhD Student

My research focuses on understanding suicide and self-harm risk among neurodivergent individuals, particularly autistic people and those with ADHD. I study how clinical, psychological, social, developmental, and healthcare-related factors may contribute to risk, and whether these factors differ across populations and settings. Using clinical records, population-based datasets, and longitudinal health data from Canada and the United States, my work aims to identify modifiable factors that can inform more targeted approaches to suicide prevention, assessment, and care.

Mishel Alexandrovsky

PhD Student

Mishel is an autistic autism researcher at the University of Toronto and the Centre for Addiction and Mental Health. She is conducting participatory work that critiques cognitive tasks commonly used in autism research. She also collaborates on studies of neurodiversity affirming care, camouflaging, and cross-neurotype evaluation. The overarching goal of her research is to improve the understanding of autistic people and, through that, help foster greater acceptance, better supports, and autistic flourishing. Alongside her research, Mishel enjoys curating collections, travelling to new and familiar places, and listening to the same songs on repeat.

Wei (William) Ai

PhD Student

I study how people navigate social expectations and manage how others see them, with a particular focus on autistic individuals. My research explores why people camouflage or hide aspects of themselves to fit in, and how this relates to mental health and social adjustment across different social situations. I also examine how autistic and non-autistic people understand one another during social interactions. I use surveys, behavioral and experimental tasks, and ecological sampling to understand how individual characteristics and social environments jointly shape these experiences.

Seavey van Walsum

Seavey van Walsum (they/them) is a Human-Centred Data Science student at the University of Toronto researching neurodiversity and social determinants of multiple health outcomes through the lens of neuroqueer and feminist theory/praxis. Their current research studies the role of collective self-esteem (CSE) for one’s gender/sex, sexuality, and neurotype on the physical, sexual, and mental health of majoritarian, single-minority, and multi-minoritarian people on the axes of gender/sex/uality and neurotype— as well as the unique differences within these populations. Their work highlights the dimensionality of the intersection of of sexual and neurodiversity minority status by comparing experiences and outcomes of majoritarian (non-LGBTQ+ neurotypical), single gender/sexual-minority (LGBTQ+ neurotypical), single neurominority (non-LGBTQ+ autistic and non-LGBTQ+ ADHD), and neuroqueer (LGBTQ+ autistic and LGBTQ+ ADHD) people.
